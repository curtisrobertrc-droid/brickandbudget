Radiator BTU Calculator UK

Work out the radiator heat output in BTU and watts needed for a room, allowing for room type, windows and insulation.

How to work it out

  1. Measure the room length, width and ceiling height in metres.
  2. The calculation converts the room volume to cubic feet and multiplies by a factor: 5 for living rooms, 4 for bedrooms and kitchens, 6 for bathrooms.
  3. Add 15% for north-facing rooms or large windows, 10% for single glazing, and 15% to 30% for average or poor insulation.
  4. Choose a radiator at or slightly above the result. Check the output at your system's flow temperature (often quoted at Delta T50).

Worked example

A 4 m by 3.5 m living room with a 2.4 m ceiling, double glazed and well insulated: about 5,930 BTU/h, or 1,740 W.

Frequently asked questions

What is Delta T50?

It is the standard test condition for radiator output. Heat pumps and low flow temperatures give less, so you may need a larger radiator.

BTU or watts?

Both measure heat output; 1 watt is about 3.412 BTU/h.

Can I use two radiators?

Yes. Split the total between them, which often heats a large room more evenly.
