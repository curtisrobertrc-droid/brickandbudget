Payments on Account Explained for Sole Traders

Why your first Self Assessment bill in January is often 150% of what you expected, and what you can do about it.

What they are

Payments on account are advance payments towards your next tax bill, including Class 4 National Insurance. There are two, due by midnight on 31 January and 31 July, and each is normally half of the tax you owed for the previous year.

When you do not have to make them

  • Your tax owed for the previous year was less than £1,000, or
  • More than 80% of the tax you owed was paid outside Self Assessment, for example through your PAYE tax code.

Your first year

If you did not make payments on account last year, the January payment covers the full bill for the year just ended plus the first payment on account for the current year. Using HMRC's own example, a £3,000 bill means £4,500 due on 31 January (£3,000 plus £1,500), then £1,500 on 31 July.

The balancing payment

If your payments on account did not cover the full bill, you pay the difference, the balancing payment, by 31 January after the tax year ends, along with the next first payment on account. If you overpaid, you may be due a refund.

Reducing them

If you expect lower profits, you can ask HMRC to reduce your payments on account through your online account or with form SA303. If you reduce them too far, HMRC charges interest on the shortfall.

Plan ahead

Use the take-home pay calculator to estimate this year's bill and how much to set aside each month. If you work under CIS, deductions already taken are credited against your bill, so keep every statement.

Keep your records straight

The TradeSheet Co CIS Tracker logs CIS deductions so you can offset them against your bill, and the Expenses & Mileage Log records costs and business miles through the year.
