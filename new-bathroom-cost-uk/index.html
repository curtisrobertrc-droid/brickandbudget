How Much Does a New Bathroom Cost in the UK? (2026)

Most UK homeowners pay between £5,500 and £8,000 for a new bathroom supplied and fitted in 2026, with an average of about £7,000. A small refresh can be done for £4,000; a large or luxury bathroom often costs £14,000 or more.

Typical 2026 price ranges

Basic refresh (like-for-like suite, minimal tiling)£4,000 to £5,500
Standard bathroom, supplied and fitted£5,500 to £8,000
Large, high-end or complex (10+ days)£14,000+
En-suite added to an existing bedroom£4,000 to £10,000
Fitting labour only, full project£1,500 to £4,000

Guides disagree: MyBuilder puts the average nearer £4,500, while Checkatrade and MyJobQuote both say about £7,000. Treat all figures as ballpark national averages; London and the South East tend to be higher, the North East lower.

How to keep the cost down

  • Keep the toilet, basin and bath or shower in the same positions to avoid moving waste pipes.
  • Tile only the wet zones and use moisture-resistant paint elsewhere.
  • Get at least three written quotes that list what is included: removal, plastering, electrics, tiling and waste.
  • Buy the suite yourself only if the fitter agrees; some will not warranty customer-supplied items.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an average new bathroom cost in the UK?

Checkatrade and MyJobQuote both publish an average of about £7,000 supplied and fitted in 2026, with most jobs between £5,500 and £8,000. High-end or complex bathrooms start at around £14,000.

How much is labour only?

Published guides put full bathroom fitting labour at roughly £1,500 to £4,000, with fitters charging about £320 to £480 per day.

How long does a new bathroom take?

Typically 5 to 10 working days, longer if the layout changes, walls are re-plastered or a wet room is tanked.

Do I need building regulations approval?

Like-for-like replacement usually does not, but new electrical circuits in a bathroom are notifiable under Part P and new drainage or ventilation must meet building regulations. Use a registered electrician who can self-certify.

Is VAT included?

Trade guide prices are usually quoted including VAT for homeowners. A VAT-registered fitter will add 20% to labour, so always ask whether a quote is inclusive.
