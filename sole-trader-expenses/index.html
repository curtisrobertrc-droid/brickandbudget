What Can a Sole Trader Claim as Expenses?

A plain-English summary of allowable expenses, based on HMRC guidance, with notes for trades.

The basic rule

Allowable expenses are running costs of the business. They reduce your taxable profit: on £40,000 turnover with £10,000 of expenses, you pay tax on £30,000. Money you take out for personal use is not an expense.

Costs you can usually claim

  • Office costs, such as stationery and phone bills
  • Travel costs, such as fuel, parking, and train or bus fares
  • Clothing such as uniforms (everyday clothes do not count)
  • Staff and subcontractor costs
  • Stock and raw materials, such as materials bought for a job
  • Financial costs, such as insurance and bank charges
  • Business premises costs, such as heating, lighting and business rates
  • Advertising and marketing, including website costs
  • Trade or professional subscriptions
  • Training related to your existing business

Tools, vans and equipment

Under traditional accounting, equipment, machinery and business vehicles are claimed as capital allowances. Under the cash basis, most items you buy and keep are claimed as expenses, except cars, which still go through capital allowances.

Mileage instead of actual costs

Instead of fuel, insurance and servicing, you can use simplified expenses: 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles in 2026/27 and 25p after. You cannot do this for a vehicle you have already claimed capital allowances on, and once you choose flat rates for a vehicle you must keep using them for it. Parking and other travel can be claimed on top.

Mixed business and personal use

Only the business share counts. If £70 of a £200 phone bill is business calls, claim £70. If you work from home, use a reasonable split, for example by rooms and time used.

The trading allowance

You cannot claim expenses or capital allowances if you use the £1,000 tax-free trading allowance instead. If you are unsure whether something is allowable, ask HMRC or an accountant.
