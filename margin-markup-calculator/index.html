Margin vs Markup Calculator UK for Trades

Price a job from its cost, or check what margin and markup a quote really gives you. A 25% margin is not a 25% markup: on a £3,000 job the difference is £250.

The formulas

  1. Profit = selling price − cost (both before VAT).
  2. Margin = profit ÷ selling price × 100.
  3. Markup = profit ÷ cost × 100.
  4. Price for a target margin = cost ÷ (1 − margin). For 25%, divide by 0.75.
  5. Price for a target markup = cost × (1 + markup). For 25%, multiply by 1.25.

Worked examples

Target margin. A job costs £3,000 and you want a 25% margin. Price = £3,000 ÷ 0.75 = £4,000. Profit £1,000, which is a 33.3% markup. Adding 25% to cost instead would give £3,750 and only a 20% margin.

Check a quote. Cost £5,430, quoted at £6,850. Profit £1,420. Margin = 1,420 ÷ 6,850 = 20.7%. Markup = 1,420 ÷ 5,430 = 26.2%.

VAT registered. Cost £800, target 30% margin. Price = £800 ÷ 0.7 = £1,142.86 before VAT. Add 20% VAT: £1,371.43 to the customer. The VAT of £228.57 goes to HMRC, so profit stays £342.86.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Both use the same profit figure. Margin divides the profit by the selling price; markup divides it by the cost. A job that costs £3,000 and is priced at £4,000 makes £1,000 profit: a 25% margin but a 33.3% markup.

How do I convert margin to markup?

Markup = margin ÷ (1 − margin). A 20% margin is 0.2 ÷ 0.8 = 25% markup. A 30% margin is 0.3 ÷ 0.7 = 42.9% markup.

How do I convert markup to margin?

Margin = markup ÷ (1 + markup). A 50% markup is 0.5 ÷ 1.5 = 33.3% margin. A 100% markup (doubling the cost) is a 50% margin.

Should VAT be included?

No. Work out margin and markup on prices before VAT. VAT is collected for HMRC and is not your profit. If you are VAT registered, add VAT at 20% after you have set the price.

What should my cost include?

Everything the job uses: materials and delivery, waste and skip hire, labour (your own time at a fair day rate as well as anyone you pay), subcontractors, plant and tool hire, and fuel. Overheads such as van, insurance and phone are often covered by adding them to the cost or by aiming for a higher margin.
